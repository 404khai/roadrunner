# Phase 14 completion

Date: 2026-10-04
Status: COMPLETE — Preparation-Time-Aware Dispatch

Phase 14 adds readiness-aware pickup timing and a pure completion-plus-idle-penalty
strategy to the existing dispatch pipeline. Phase 13's Basic Dispatch remains available
with its zero-wait road-travel objective. Domain ownership, rider eligibility, candidate
coverage, routing/anchor validation, feasibility, immutable decisions, atomic commit,
and shared readiness/pickup/delivery transitions are reused.

The public entry point is `preparation_aware_dispatch`, configured with a validated
`PreparationAwareStrategy`. The existing `OrderReadiness.expected_at` stores expected
ready time; an observed ready event takes precedence. Neither value present produces
`DispatchEvaluationError::MissingReadiness`. Waiting occurs at pickup and advances the
onward routing departure. Service remains zero. See
[dispatch usage](dispatch.md#preparation-aware-dispatch-phase-14) and
[ADR 0014](adr/0014-preparation-aware-dispatch.md) for assumptions and scoring semantics.

## Requirement review

| Phase 14 requirement | Implemented behavior | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expected ready time | Existing mutable forecast, separate from observation; observation wins | `PreparationPolicyEvidence`; readiness precedence/absence fixture |
| Preparation-aware timing | Wait until effective readiness when arrival is early; no negative wait | Shared `StopTimeline`; past/equal/intermediate readiness fixtures |
| Completion plus rider idle penalty | `completion_time + weight × waiting`, default weight 1.0 | Pure `PreparationAwareStrategy`; configurable tradeoff and exact-order fixtures |
| Pickup ETA | Road pickup travel plus separate arrival and departure instants | Per-candidate `PlanEvaluation` and serialized decision evidence |
| Wait time | Separate pickup stop and aggregate waiting durations | Long-preparation scenario and boundary fixtures |
| Delivery ETA | Delivery travel routed at departure after waiting | Recording provider with real FIFO traffic and nonzero routing epoch |
| Total completion time | Explicit elapsed duration and absolute completed-dropoff instant | `PlanEvaluation.completion_time`; soft deadline fixture |
| Explain preference | Readiness facts/source, weight, completion/wait/penalty contributions, tie reason | `DecisionEvidence.preparation` and `ScoreContributions.preparation` |
| Simulate why proximity can lose | Fresh equivalent worlds, fixed external ready events, executed fulfillment | Three runnable synthetic scenarios and forecast-error fixture |
| Preserve world safety | Readiness revisions make proposals stale; actual pickup needs a ready observation | Shared commit/custody fixture; atomic failures without mutation |

## Reproducible comparisons

```bash
cargo run -p roadrunner-dispatch --example preparation_aware_dispatch --locked
```

The [example](../crates/roadrunner-dispatch/examples/preparation_aware_dispatch.rs)
executes both strategies through shared world transitions. Both actual road legs use
the same fixed graph/provider, and completed pickup/delivery times are read from
recorded fulfillment. Output separates predicted decisions and observed synthetic
execution metrics. Every run starts from the same fresh request, rider states, and
external ready event; all orders finish and no population is dropped.

The generated observations are:

| Scenario | Baseline rider / pickup wait | Preparation-aware rider / pickup wait | Completed delivery duration, both strategies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long preparation: ready after 18 min | Rider 1 / 13 min | Rider 2 / 3 min | 28 min |
| Already ready | Rider 1 / 0 min | Rider 1 / 0 min | 15 min |
| Forecast 18 min, actually ready after 25 min | Rider 1 / 20 min | Rider 2 / 10 min | 35 min |

Rider 1 is geographically nearer and reaches pickup after 5 minutes. Rider 2 arrives
after 15 minutes. The fixed delivery road takes 10 minutes. These are synthetic
correctness observations generated by the example and verified by tests, not a
benchmark or a general performance claim. A separate tradeoff fixture proves that
positive waiting penalties can prefer a later delivery with less idle time; zero
weight minimizes readiness-aware completion duration alone.

## Scope boundary

All candidates are available idle riders and depart at the evaluation instant.
This phase does not schedule delayed departures or insert orders into busy rider plans.
Predicted readiness is not an actual event, and no new SLA constraint is introduced.

The small deterministic scenario executions are caller-driven. Phase 15's general
simulation clock/event queue, Phase 16's fleet strategy benchmarks, multi-order insertion,
re-dispatch, HTTP, persistence, production data, ML, and UI remain deferred. No new
runtime latency, memory, scalability, or production-efficiency claim is made.
